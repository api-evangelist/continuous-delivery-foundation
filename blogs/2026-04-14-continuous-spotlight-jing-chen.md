	<h3>What&#8217;s your favourite thing/project/tech to work on?</h3>
<p>The thing I’m most proud of is getting back into coding and contributing to open source after not doing it for many years. Over the last year, I pushed myself to become technically active again, contribute consistently, and reconnect with a part of my career that I had not used in a long time.</p>
<p>That journey meant a lot to me because coming back after such a long break was not easy. I had to relearn, adapt, and rebuild my confidence. Being awarded several Ortelius badges, including Champion, Legend, and Ambassador recognition, made that journey feel real. Those badges were meaningful because they showed that my contributions were valued by the community.</p>
<p>More than the awards themselves, I’m proud of what they represent: persistence, growth, and the willingness to start again. For me, this was about proving that it is never too late to contribute, learn new things, and make a difference.</p>

	<h3>Tell us about the thing you’re most proud of and why?</h3>
<p>One of the things I’m most proud of is helping build the Ortelius open source community, not just the technology, but the people who make the project thrive.</p>
<p>Open source can be an incredibly powerful way to innovate, but historically, it hasn’t always been the most welcoming place for everyone. Women in particular are still significantly underrepresented in open-source projects. From the beginning of Ortelius, I made a conscious effort to change that dynamic by encouraging women to participate, contribute, and step into leadership roles within the community.</p>
<p>Over time, that effort has paid off in ways that mean more to me than any technical milestone. We now have an amazing group of women who contribute to Ortelius in meaningful ways, writing code, producing documentation, leading outreach, organizing events, and helping shape the direction of the project. They’ve become an integral part of the community and the culture we’ve built.</p>
<p>Seeing these contributors grow in confidence, influence, and leadership has been incredibly rewarding. Open source works best when it reflects the diversity of the people who rely on the technology.<br />
For me, the Ortelius community proves that when you intentionally create space for people to participate, great things happen, for the project and for the people involved.</p>

<div class="wpb_text_column wpb_content_element ">
	<h3>What is your #1 tip for getting involved in the community?</h3>
<p>Just get involved. Listen, learn, and see where you can contribute. You do not need to start with a big technical contribution — small, consistent steps matter. Whether it is documentation, reviewing, organising ideas, giving feedback, helping with outreach, or simply showing up regularly, every contribution counts. Open source communities grow through participation, and once people see your genuine interest and reliability, more opportunities naturally follow.</p>

	<h3>What&#8217;s your favourite open source conference?</h3>
<p>I haven’t attended one in person yet, but cdCon is at the top of my list. I’m especially drawn to it because it brings together people focused on delivery, DevOps, and practical software improvement. I also value open source communities like Ortelius, where I’ve been involved in discussions and planning around events and collaboration. What appeals to me most about conferences like cdCon is that the conversations are grounded in real systems, real challenges, and shared learning. I really enjoy conferences where the discussions are not just theoretical, but focused on real challenges, real systems, and real community collaboration.</p>
